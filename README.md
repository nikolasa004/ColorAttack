# Color Attack

<p align="center">
<img src="docs/images/main-screen.jpg" alt="Color Attack main menu" width="270">
</p>

<p align="center"><strong>A tiny 2D high-school project that somehow turned into five worlds, six minigames, a city, shops, stories, and a lot of drawing.</strong></p>

I started **Color Attack** in my final year of high school, when the plan was to make a very small game about matching colors as shapes fell down the screen. That was supposed to be pretty much it. A few years, many pauses, and a ridiculous number of new ideas later, the project had become something much bigger.

This is still a personal hobby game rather than a polished commercial release. It is also the one game I keep coming back to, adding another animation, another character, another mechanic, or another little story.

## At a glance

- **5 main campaign levels**, each in a different environment and with its own mechanics.
- **Competitive mode** for replaying unlocked levels, earning diamonds, and chasing leaderboard scores.
- **6 minigames**: five unlocked through progression and one hidden in the market storyline.
- **A city, an evolving house, three shopkeepers, dialogues, and a lot of little details.**
- **20 level-specific totems**, **6 potions**, and **dozens of skins** (30 of them are shown in this README).
- Hand-drawn characters, backgrounds, level variants, and plenty of animations.

## How it works

The basic idea is simple: **watch the falling shapes and react with the right colors.** Red, blue, and yellow are only the beginning. Mixed colors add another layer of challenge, and each new world introduces something that changes how you play. The controls stay approachable, but keeping up gets harder.

### Campaign and competitive

| Campaign | Competitive |
| --- | --- |
| Progress through five increasingly different levels. | Replay levels already cleared in the campaign. |
| Earn **coins** for potions and totems. | Earn **diamonds** to buy character skins. |
| Completing a level opens up the matching competitive level and more things around the game. | Aim for better scores and appear on the **online leaderboards**. |

There are separate scores for the two modes. The online leaderboards display results by level and depend on the external leaderboard service being available.

### The five worlds

1. **Level 1 - The basics.** A deliberately simpler starting point that teaches the main color-and-shape mechanics.
2. **Level 2 - The grassy world.** A brighter, greener environment where hearts are introduced and the game starts adding more to keep track of.
3. **Level 3 - Snow and ice.** Falling snow and icy shapes complicate the basic gameplay, with their own hazards and effects.
4. **Level 4 - Underwater.** A sea full of colorful fish, including tricky combinations such as red-blue and blue-red fish. They can represent the same mixed color but look different at a glance.
5. **Level 5 - Outer space.** Break open special meteors to find shapes or crystals. Collecting all three special crystals unlocks additional advantages.

And yes, each level has its own portal, visuals, animations, scoring, and a set of totems. The portals alone took more drawing than the entire original game was supposed to need.

### Unlocking more than levels

Progression is not limited to the campaign menu. Completing levels also unlocks **minigames** and new things for the **house**. It starts fairly empty and gradually becomes a place filled with souvenirs and objects, including a pet, an aquarium, sledding equipment, and other reminders of the worlds you have visited.

The house is also where you can browse your skins. Some of my favorite details are not part of the main gameplay at all; they are things you only notice when you return to the city after making progress.

### Six minigames

The main game eventually became big enough that I started making smaller games inside it:

- **Memory game:** a classic memory challenge.
- **Cheese Ninja:** slice flying pieces of cheese, with a little nod to Fruit Ninja.
- **Sledding:** race through the snow while dodging trees.
- **Fishing:** catch fish with a rod in the underwater-themed minigame.
- **UFO puzzle:** a grid-based challenge with a Minesweeper-like feel.
- **Pizza minigame:** an extra activity connected to the market and its characters.

The first five tie into the campaign progression and house. The pizza game is part of the shop side of the game, and it has its own little setup.

### Shops, currencies, and upgrades

The market has **three different sellers**, each with their own personality and dialogue:

- **Potions** are bought with coins. They affect gameplay in different ways, and more become available as you progress. Many can be bought repeatedly to stack or change their effects.
- **Totems** are bought with coins. Each level has its own set, for **20 totems in total**, ranging from helpful bonuses to mechanics that change which obstacles appear.
- **Skins** are bought with diamonds earned in competitive play. There are far more than the 30 shown in the gallery below.

I originally imagined a tiny shop with just a few items. Naturally, that turned into multiple sellers, five separate totem shelves, new potion unlocks, and a whole skin system.

### Characters and dialogue

There is a surprising amount of dialogue in this game. The opening introduces the world gradually, and the city, house, and shopkeepers have their own conversations and events. Some of the characters even became recurring parts of the game instead of just being decorative NPCs.

I spent a lot of time on their appearances, the transitions, the little animations, and the way different parts of the game connect. If you only watch one gameplay clip, you will probably miss most of it.

## How this happened

When I first made Color Attack for my **high-school graduation project**, there was only supposed to be one level. You started the game, shapes fell from above, and you pressed the matching buttons. Simple, finished, move on.

Then I thought mixed colors would be more interesting. So I added purple, orange, and green. Then I wanted better animations. Then I drew a grassy environment and a UFO that would fly into the scene.

At some point I decided that one level was not enough. The grassy environment became the second level, the UFO eventually found its place in the fifth, and I had to make a completely new first level. I kept coming up with ideas for different environments, special mechanics, a shop, and upgrades. A few totems became twenty. The shop grew its own cast of characters. Those characters needed dialogue. The dialogue needed events. The events needed more animations. And so on.

The skins were another adventure. Drawing a new character was only the first part, because I also wanted different artwork and variations that would fit the different level environments. Once you do that for a lot of characters, even a tiny cosmetic feature stops being tiny.

I worked on Color Attack on and off over several years, sometimes leaving it alone for a long time and then returning to it with another idea. It is **still a work in progress**, largely because I kept expanding it and spending an enormous amount of time drawing and animating things.

It is not the most technically complicated project I have ever written. That is not really the point. It is a little game that slowly turned into its own colorful world, and I wanted to keep that history visible.

## Download and repository notes

**Android:** The playable APK is distributed through the **Releases** section of this repository. **[Download Color Attack for Android (APK)](https://github.com/nikolasa004/ColorAttack/releases/tag/v0.1.0)**

The source project is made with **Unity and C#**. It contains the game code and project settings, but certain third-party assets (including some fonts, sound effects, music, and Unity Asset Store resources) are intentionally excluded from the repository. Because of that, a fresh clone may **not** be immediately buildable without the separately obtained assets. The release APK is the simplest way to try the game.

This repository is a record of an ongoing personal game, not a finished product or a reusable starter template.

## Final note

Color Attack started with a few colored shapes falling down a screen. It grew into five worlds, six minigames, a shop, a house, dozens of skins, and far more dialogue and drawings than I ever planned.

I do not know when I will finally call it finished. But after all the time I have spent on it, I wanted to share what it has become.
